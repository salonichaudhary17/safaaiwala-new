# Field Research & User Persona Report
**Project Safaaiwala - Smart India Hackathon**

## Overview
As per the requirement to bridge the informational and institutional gap in the e-waste management sector, our team conducted on-ground field research at **Shalimar Bagh, Wazirpur Industrial Area**. We interviewed two local scrap collectors (Kabadiwalas) and a formal recycler to understand their daily operations, pricing knowledge, handling practices, and hesitations regarding the formal recycling ecosystem.

---

## Interview 1: The Last-Mile Collector
**Name:** Ram Prasad (Name changed for privacy)  
**Profile:** Local Kabadiwala (Push-cart operator)  
**Location:** Wazirpur Industrial Area / Shalimar Bagh Sector  

### Key Observations & Pain Points:
1. **Price Asymmetry:** Ram Prasad relies entirely on the rate dictated by his local scrap yard boss. He is unaware that materials like PCBs (Printed Circuit Boards) have different grades and ends up selling high-value lithium batteries and copper-bearing motors at a flat "mixed scrap" rate.
2. **Hazardous Practices:** To extract copper quickly, he occasionally resorts to open-air burning of insulated wires. He is unaware of the severe health risks of dioxin smoke.
3. **Digital Illiteracy & Trust:** He uses a basic Android smartphone but does not use UPI frequently due to a lack of a formal bank account. He prefers daily cash payouts to feed his family.

### How Safaaiwala Solves This:
- **Audio-Visual UI:** Voice assistant and TTS in regional languages allows him to use the app without needing to read complex text.
- **Cash-First Ledger:** He can record the exact weight and value of his collection digitally while still accepting physical cash.

---

## Interview 2: The E-Rickshaw Collector
**Name:** Shyam Yadav (Name changed for privacy)  
**Profile:** Kabadiwala (E-Rickshaw operator covering residential zones)  
**Location:** Shalimar Bagh  

### Key Observations & Pain Points:
1. **Logistics & Matching:** Shyam collects larger volumes (e.g., heavy CRT monitors and old washing machines) but wastes fuel driving around trying to find which authorized recycler is currently accepting specific items.
2. **No Financial History:** Despite moving thousands of rupees in scrap weekly, his entire business runs on a paper notebook (Bahi-Khata). He cannot get a micro-loan to buy a larger tempo because he has no formal financial history.

### How Safaaiwala Solves This:
- **Recycler Matching:** The app uses GPS to match his specific lot (e.g., CRT Monitors) to a recycler currently accepting them within his radius.
- **Micro-Passbook:** The digital ledger builds a verifiable history of his transactions, which can eventually be used to prove creditworthiness.

---

## Interview 3: The Formal Sector Representative
**Entity:** E Waste Recycler Hub  
**Profile:** Authorized CPCB Recycler Facility  
**Location:** Wazirpur Industrial Area  

### Key Observations & Pain Points:
1. **Compliance Burden:** The recycler expressed that buying directly from informal kabadiwalas is a compliance nightmare because generating transfer manifests and verifying the chain-of-custody for EPR (Extended Producer Responsibility) targets requires structured data that kabadiwalas cannot provide.
2. **Fear of Fraud:** They face issues with informal aggregators inflating weights to claim higher payouts.

### How Safaaiwala Solves This:
- **Automated Traceability & Anti-Fraud:** Safaaiwala's SHA-256 digital handover receipt completely automates the CPCB compliance paperwork for the recycler, while the On-Device Anti-Fraud engine blocks impossible weight entries.

---

## Conclusion
Our field research in Wazirpur confirmed that the primary barriers to formalization are a lack of transparency, complex compliance, and language barriers. Safaaiwala's offline-first, vernacular, and cash-tolerant architecture directly addresses these exact pain points.
