# Field Research & User Persona Report
**Project Safaaiwala - Smart India Hackathon**

## Overview
As per the requirement to bridge the informational and institutional gap in the e-waste management sector, our team conducted on-ground field research with local scrap collectors (Kabadiwalas) and mid-level aggregators. The objective was to understand their daily operations, pricing knowledge, handling practices, and hesitations regarding the formal recycling ecosystem.

---

## Interview 1: The Informal Last-Mile Collector
**Name:** Ram Prasad (Name changed for privacy)  
**Profile:** Local Kabadiwala (Push-cart operator)  
**Location:** Urban residential sector  
**Daily Collection:** 15-20 kg of mixed scrap (incl. 2-3 kg of e-waste)

### Key Observations & Pain Points:
1. **Price Asymmetry:** Ram Prasad relies entirely on the rate dictated by his local scrap yard boss. He is unaware that materials like PCBs (Printed Circuit Boards) have different grades (e.g., green boards vs. brown boards) and end up selling high-value lithium batteries and copper-bearing motors at a flat "mixed plastic/metal" rate.
2. **Hazardous Practices:** To extract copper quickly, he occasionally resorts to open-air burning of insulated wires. When asked why, he stated that mechanical stripping takes too much time, and he is unaware of the severe health risks of dioxin smoke.
3. **Digital Illiteracy & Trust:** He uses a basic Android smartphone primarily for WhatsApp and YouTube. He does not use UPI frequently due to a lack of a formal bank account and prefers daily cash payouts to feed his family.

### How Safaaiwala Solves This:
- **Audio-Visual UI:** Voice assistant and TTS in Hindi/Marathi allows him to use the app without needing to read complex text.
- **Safety Prompts:** The app visually and audibly warns him against burning wires and explains that recyclers will pay full price for unburnt cables.
- **Cash-First Ledger:** He can record the exact weight and value of his collection digitally while still accepting physical cash from the aggregator.

## Interview 2: The Mid-Level Aggregator
**Name:** Kishan Lal Scrap Network  
**Profile:** Small Scrap Yard Owner & Tempo Driver  
**Location:** Semi-urban industrial border  
**Daily Volume:** 200-500 kg of sorted scrap

### Key Observations & Pain Points:
1. **Compliance Burden:** Kishan knows about authorized CPCB recyclers, but the paperwork required to generate transfer manifests is too complex. He prefers selling to larger informal scrap mafias because "no questions are asked."
2. **Logistics & Matching:** He doesn't know which authorized recycler needs which specific material on a given day. He spends a lot of money on fuel driving his tempo to a recycler, only to find out they aren't accepting CRT monitors that week.
3. **No Financial History:** Despite moving lakhs of rupees in scrap monthly, his entire business runs on a paper notebook (Bahi-Khata). He cannot get a micro-loan to buy a larger truck because he has no formal financial history.

### How Safaaiwala Solves This:
- **Automated Traceability:** Safaaiwala's SHA-256 digital handover receipt completely automates the CPCB compliance paperwork.
- **Recycler Matching:** The app uses GPS to match his specific lot (e.g., CRT Monitors) to a recycler currently accepting them within a 10km radius.
- **Micro-Passbook:** The digital ledger builds a verifiable history of his transactions, which can eventually be used to prove creditworthiness for micro-finance loans.

---

## Conclusion
The field research confirmed that the primary barriers to formalization are not a lack of willingness, but a lack of transparency, complex compliance, and language barriers. Safaaiwala's offline-first, vernacular, and cash-tolerant architecture directly addresses these pain points.
